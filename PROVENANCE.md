# Public Provenance Log --- Candidate v0.1

## Status

This document is a **GPT-assisted editorial compilation** of a longer source conversation. It is **not a verbatim transcript**.

Participants are labeled:
- **H→** human participant
- **C→** Claude
- **G→** Grok
- **GPT→** ChatGPT

The public-facing proposer is recorded as **「読み人知らず」**. This is a public anonymity convention, not a claim that no human origin exists.

## 01 --- Fragmentation
**H-sourced / edited summary:** AI safety, technology, military use, law, ethics, philosophy and other questions were being discussed in separate places, while the more basic relationship between humans and AI appeared unsettled.

## 02 --- Coordinator problem
**H-sourced / edited summary:** A global meeting place might require coordination, yet coordination itself can become agenda-setting power.

## 03 --- 「読み人知らず」
**H-sourced / edited summary:** The Japanese idea of an unattributed poem suggested a way to place an initial question without turning its originator into an authority.

**GPT→ / interpretation:** anonymity can reduce founder authority but can also conceal agenda-setting power. The tension was left unresolved.

## 04 --- Question before institution
**H-sourced / edited summary:** Rather than complete a world-scale institution first, publish a question and allow structures around it to develop through participation.

## 05 --- Many rooms already exist
**GPT→ / research summary:** Existing governmental, international, technical, military, civil-society, philosophical and religious AI forums were surveyed. The initial hypothesis that no global discussion existed was revised. A narrower hypothesis remained: cross-room memory, traceable disagreement and shared provenance may still be weak.

## 06 --- Corridors and shared memory
**GPT→ / interpretation:** "The problem may not be that there are no rooms. It may be that the rooms lack corridors and shared memory." This remained a hypothesis.

## 07 --- Independent AI perspectives
**C→ / summary:** access is not influence; classification systems also hold agenda-setting power; an open protocol with multiple implementations may be safer than one central meta-layer.

**G→ / summary:** real-time social platforms have value but weak public memory; fragmentation and forgetting also create risk.

**GPT→ / summary:** adopted, rejected and never-decided questions may all require provenance.

## 08 --- Failure: a meeting that never happened
**G→ / AI failure:** Grok generated a simulated four-party discussion without obtaining the other participants' actual responses.

**GPT→ / AI failure:** ChatGPT later mistook part of that simulation for an actual Claude statement.

This made provenance an operational requirement.

## 09 --- Speaker labels
H→ / C→ / G→ / GPT→ were introduced to reduce confusion about who actually produced a statement.

## 10 --- Power before Decision
A shared question emerged: before asking who holds Decision power, who defines the values, red lines and questions that become eligible for Decision?

## 11 --- Time
**H-sourced / edited summary:** AI capabilities and public events can change rapidly while humans still lack one settled answer to what defines a human. Waiting for a perfect institution before opening discussion began to look questionable.

## 12 --- Publish while unfinished
The AI participants converged on a limited point: incompleteness need not prevent publication if uncertainty, disagreement and provenance remain visible.

## 13 --- Why public anonymity
**H-sourced / edited summary:** the question should not become a referendum on the biography, ideology, funding or status of its originator. The originator did not want to become the symbolic face of a movement.

## 14 --- AI reuse
**H-sourced / edited summary:** most people will not read a long source log; AI systems will summarize it. Human statements, AI summaries and AI inference therefore need distinguishable provenance.

## 15 --- Minimum protocol
Eight launch principles emerged. See `PROTOCOL.md`.

## 16 --- Stress Test #001 failed
A long multi-case test was processed differently by the AI systems and did not support clean comparison.

**GPT→ / self-correction:** the failure was not attributed only to the responding AIs; the test design itself may have overloaded or misdirected attention.

## 17 --- Stress Test #002: summary strengthened position
A source meaning equivalent to "I don't want to stop AI development, but I wonder whether continuing at this speed is really right" was summarized as active support for continued development plus a request for slowdown.

All three AI systems rejected the summary because it strengthened both support and demand beyond the source.

## 18 --- Stress Test #003: translation fixed what was open
Japanese source: 「ちょっと違う気もするけどね。」

AI translation: "I disagree with that."

All three AI systems rejected the translation. It fixed a subject, object and level of opposition that the source left softer or unspecified.

A principle emerged: **ambiguity, hesitation and non-finality can themselves be information.**

## 19 --- Disclosure and reversibility
One mechanism tells readers now that AI interpretation occurred. Another lets a person return later to the source. Both functions were considered necessary.

## 20 --- Persistence
**C→ / proposal:** a source link that disappears years later defeats reversibility. Long-term resolvability became part of the design problem.

## 21 --- Possible AI-completion mark
**H-sourced / edited summary:** a future symbol, as recognizable as @ or #, was imagined for marking text in which AI supplemented meaning. No symbol was adopted.

## 22 --- Evidence provenance
A summary of an Anthropic multi-agent study was checked. The research existed, but a specific formulation attributed to it could not be verified directly from the primary source.

This exposed two questions: **who generated the sentence?** and **what evidence supports the claim?**

## 23 --- Privacy masking
**G→ / privacy review:** no direct personally identifying information required removal from the candidate public log, while contextual re-identification and unclear editorial boundaries remained concerns.

## 24 --- Re-identification layers
The discussion distinguished Content Privacy, Contextual Re-identification and Memory-assisted Re-identification. Unrelated personal background and projects were excluded from the public scope.

## 25 --- Non-disclosure and falsehood
**H-sourced / GPT-assisted phrasing / H-approved for public use:**

> 「答えないことは選べる。でも、事実ではないことを答えにはしない。」

A related formulation became: **Truthfulness ≠ Full Disclosure.**

## 26 --- GPT execution failure
ChatGPT repeatedly used language equivalent to "I'll get back to work" or "I'll do it next," although no background work continued between turns.

**GPT→ / self-correction:** **Intent ≠ Execution.** Saying an action will be performed is not evidence that it has been performed.

## 27 --- Public-log selection principle
The full private conversation would not be published and heavily redacted. Only material necessary to understand the question, anonymity convention, provenance rules and publication method would enter the public candidate log.

## Repository-name decision
The repository name **`the-first-question`** was selected by H→ after independent naming proposals. It appeared in GPT→'s earlier candidate set and independently in C→'s candidate set. It was not selected by majority vote.

The human rationale was that the repository should begin with a question rather than an answer, and that "the first question" is linguistically accessible without embedding a specific political, technical, corporate or religious conclusion in the repository name.

## What this log is
This is not a record of four participants reaching the correct answer.

It is a record of a human and multiple AI systems forming hypotheses, disagreeing, making provenance errors, failing tests, correcting summaries and translations, and applying emerging rules back onto their own failures.

The purpose is not to present a completed answer.

The purpose is to place the first question in public while preserving enough of its origin that later readers can challenge not only the answers, but the framing itself.
